Understanding Soil Moisture Sensors

Posted on August 12, 2026 by R. Green

Soil moisture sensors let a drip-irrigation controller decide when to water instead of watering on a fixed timer. This guide covers the two dominant sensing technologies, how to wire them into an ESP32, and where growers commonly place them wrong.

Figure 1 — typical placement depth is 5–7 cm for shallow-rooted vegetables.

Resistive vs. Capacitive

Resistive

  • Cheap, often under $2 per unit
  • Corrodes within one growing season
  • Reading drifts as corrosion progresses

Capacitive

  • Higher unit cost, roughly $6–$10
  • No exposed electrodes, so no corrosion
  • Stable readings across multiple seasons

Reading the Sensor from an ESP32

The probe outputs an analog voltage proportional to moisture. Wire the signal pin to an ADC-capable GPIO and average several samples to reduce noise:

const int SENSOR_PIN = 34;

int readMoisturePercent() {
  long total = 0;
  for (int i = 0; i < 10; i++) {
    total += analogRead(SENSOR_PIN);
    delay(10);
  }
  int raw = total / 10;
  // Calibrate DRY_VALUE and WET_VALUE for your specific probe.
  return map(raw, DRY_VALUE, WET_VALUE, 0, 100);
}

Old greenhouse saying

Once calibrated, set your controller to irrigate when the reading drops below roughly 35% for most vegetable beds, adjusting for species and pot size.

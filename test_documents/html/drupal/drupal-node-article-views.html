Retrofitting a Rain Garden for Clay Soil

Submitted by M. Alvarez on Wed, 08/12/2026 - 09:00

Clay soil drains slowly, which is exactly the problem a rain garden is supposed to solve. Rather than abandoning the site, amend the basin and route the outflow so standing water never lingers more than 24 hours.

Diagnosing Drainage Before You Dig

Dig a test hole 30 cm deep and fill it with water. If it hasn't drained within 24 hours, you have a percolation problem that grading alone will not fix — you'll need an underdrain.

Amending the Basin

  • Remove the top 45 cm of native clay across the basin footprint
  • Backfill with a 60/20/20 mix of sand, compost, and the original topsoil
  • Install a perforated underdrain wrapped in filter fabric at the base

Plant Selection for Wet-Dry Cycling

Choose species tolerant of both saturated soil after storms and drought between them. Swamp milkweed, blue flag iris, and switchgrass all handle the swing well in most temperate zones.

A rain garden is not a pond and not a bog — it's designed to be dry most of the time.

Sizing the Basin

A common rule of thumb sizes the basin to roughly 20% of the contributing impervious area for a 60 cm amended depth. Shallower amendment depths need a proportionally larger footprint to hold the same storm volume.

Amendment depthBasin area (% of impervious source)
30 cm35%
45 cm25%
60 cm20%

These figures assume a native clay subgrade with an infiltration rate under 2 mm/hr; sandier subgrades can shrink the basin further.

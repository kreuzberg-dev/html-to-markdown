Field Notes: A Weekend Kiln Build

We built a small propane raku kiln out of a 55-gallon drum and ceramic fiber blanket over a single weekend. Total cost came in under $180, and it reaches cone 06 in about forty minutes.

This post walks through the materials list, the fiber-lining technique that keeps the drum from radiating heat like a furnace, and the burner port placement that made the biggest difference in even heating.

The fiber blanket compresses to about half its loose thickness once tucked and pinned.

Materials

  • One 55-gallon steel drum, open top
  • Ceramic fiber blanket, 1 inch, 8 lb density
  • High-temperature rigidizer spray
  • Single-port propane forge burner

Burner Placement

Angle the burner port slightly tangential to the drum wall rather than pointing straight at the center — this creates a swirling flame path that heats the chamber far more evenly than a direct blast.

First Firing Results

Cone 06 dropped at roughly the 38-minute mark on our first run, a bit faster than expected for a kiln this size. We're attributing that to the tangential burner placement more than anything else.

Next build: a second port on the opposite wall to even out the hot spot near the flue.

Do I need a kiln shelter?

Yes for rain, but propane raku firing is loud and produces open flame at the burner port, so an open-sided shelter works better than a fully enclosed shed.

How long does the fiber lining last?

With the rigidizer coating, ours has held up for over sixty firings with no visible degradation of the fiber surface.

Can this design reach stoneware temperatures?

Not reliably. A single-port 55-gallon drum kiln is built for raku-range firing; stoneware cone 6 and above needs better insulation and typically a second burner.
